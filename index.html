Nia Thompson Week 12 Update

During a follow-up visit, Nia says that for the past three weeks she has occasionally heard her name called when no one appears to be nearby. She also reports feeling watched at work and says two coworkers may be sending her coded messages through ordinary comments. She is unsure whether this interpretation is correct but has begun avoiding the break room.

Nia has slept about three to four hours per night for the past week while working extra shifts. Her partner reports that Nia sometimes changes topics quickly and seems harder to follow. Nia used cannabis twice during the past month and recently restarted a prescribed medication, but the type and timing are not provided.

Nia remains oriented, arrives on time, expresses concern about the changes, and agrees to discuss safety and medical evaluation. She denies current intent to harm herself or anyone else. The case does not establish duration beyond the details above, prior episodes, mood-episode timing, intoxication or withdrawal, medical symptoms, trauma cues, family history, cultural or spiritual meaning, or decline across major areas of functioning.

Professional boundary: This fictional information raises important assessment priorities. It does not establish schizophrenia, dangerousness, or any other diagnosis.
Experience What was perceived, believed, said, reported, or observed?
Course When did it begin, how often does it occur, and what changed?
Meaning How does Nia understand the experience, and what context matters?
